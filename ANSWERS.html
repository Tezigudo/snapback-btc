🧮 Algorithms used (in plain English)

1. Donchian Breakout (the one that works in trends)

Step 1: Look at the last 40 bars (40 × 4 hours = 6.7 days). Find the highest HIGH and lowest LOW.
Step 2: If price closes ABOVE the 40-bar high → BUY (long). Bet that the breakout continues up.
Step 3: If price closes BELOW the 40-bar low → SELL (short). Bet that the breakdown continues down.
Step 4 (exit): Close when price hits the opposite 10-bar channel, OR when it moves 1.5×ATR against us (stop loss).
Step 5 (NEW in v3 — regime gate): Only take the trade if a long EMA (200-period) is moving in the same direction. This refuses to short during an uptrend, or long during a downtrend.

Why it works in trends: in a trending market, a new high usually means more new highs are coming.

Why it fails in chop: in a sideways market, a "breakout" is usually just noise — price reverses back inside the channel.

2. Snapback (DEAD — no edge)

Buy when RSI(2) drops below 10 (oversold), short when RSI(2) goes above 90 (overbought). Plus 1h EMA trend filter + volume confirmation + funding bias.

Why it failed: BTC at 15m bars doesn't actually mean-revert. Strategy buys "oversold" → keeps falling. Lost 5%+ in walk-forward.

3. Carry (DEAD — no edge)

Every 8 hours, Binance Futures charges a "funding rate". When it's positive, longs pay shorts. We bet AGAINST the crowd to collect the fee.

Why it failed: the fee is tiny (~0.01-0.1% per 8h), but directional losses are large (1-5% per trade). In a bull market, funding is mostly positive → strategy is mostly short → gets crushed on rallies. Lost 18.6% per year compounded.

4. Funding Momentum (DEAD — wrong direction)

Inverse of carry: trade WITH the crowd. If funding is rising rapidly (longs piling in), go long.

Why it failed: the crowd is wrong as often as right. With 1% SL and 2% TP, you need >33% win rate just to break even. We didn't get there. Lost 66% in-sample alone.

5. Ensemble (DEAD — falsified)

Run Donchian + Carry at 50/50 capital split. Hope they offset each other.

Why it failed: the two strategies aren't actually uncorrelated. They both lose in the same bad months (Oct 2024, late 2023). Combined Sharpe was −0.30, worse than either alone.

⏰ 15m vs 4h — is this day trading?

Short answer: No, neither timeframe is strictly "day trading".

  • 15m timeframe = look at price every 15 minutes. We used this for snapback & carry. Trades typically lasted hours to a few days. NOT day trading (which means open + close same day).
  • 4h timeframe = look at price every 4 hours. We use this for Donchian-v3 (the working one). Trades typically last 1-7 days. This is "swing trading", not day trading.

The 4h timeframe is the right one for our working strategy. Looking at the passing test:

  • Average hold: ~4-5 days
  • Longest hold: 10.3 days (one big winning short during the May 2022 crash)
  • Shortest holds: 0h (intra-bar stop-outs, ~3 of 15 trades)

You wake up, check positions once or twice a day, the bot does the rest. Not glued to the screen.

⏳ Max 1-month hold rule

You asked: "future position doesnt need to wait so long at maximum of 1 month".

Done. Added time_stop_bars=180 to Donchian (= 30 days at 4h timeframe). Any position open longer than 30 days will be force-closed.

In practice: looking at all trades above, the longest hold was 10.3 days. So the rule won't trigger often — but it's now a safety net against pathological "position held for 6 months because no exit signal fired" scenarios.
