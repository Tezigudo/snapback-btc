multifactor-v1 Confluence on 15-minute bars

The four gates (LONG variant)

Gate 1 — Oversold short-term
RSI(14) on 15m close < 40

Standard 14-period RSI. Threshold is 40 (not 30) — we want noticeable weakness but not panic. RSI(14)<40 happens often, so by itself it's noise.

Gate 2 — Higher-timeframe trend up
close > EMA(200) on 15m

200 bars × 15min = 50 hours of trend context. Filters out "buying the dip" in a downtrend (the #1 newbie killer). If close < EMA(200), no longs allowed.

Gate 3 — Real volume conviction
current volume > 2.0 × SMA(20-bar volume)

20 × 15min = 5 hours of baseline volume. The dip bar must have ≥2× that volume. Filters out low-liquidity wicks that look like dips on the chart but had no real selling. This is what separates a real signal from chart noise.

Gate 4 — Funding not extreme
funding_rate ≤ +0.0500% / 8h

If longs are paying shorts more than +0.05%/8h, the long side is over-crowded. Block new longs. This is the "don't fight positioning" filter — extreme funding often marks the top, not a dip-to-buy.

Edge thesis: any one filter alone loses money. Their conjunction wins because the false signals get rejected. Out of (say) 100 bars where RSI<40 fires, maybe 30 also have close>EMA(200), maybe 15 of those also have 2× volume, and maybe 10 of those also have non-extreme funding. Those 10 are the high-confidence setups. Mirror logic for shorts (RSI>70, close<EMA(200), etc.).
Failure modes:
  • Chop above EMA(200) with no follow-through: TP at +3% rarely hits, SL at −1.5% does
  • News-driven gap below entry: 1.5% SL gets blown through; actual loss is bigger
  • Trending market where EMA(200) is the support that's about to crack — buys the last dip before the trend break

donchian-v3 cons Trend-confirmed breakout on 4-hour bars

The two gates (LONG variant)

Gate 1 — Donchian channel breakout
close > max(high, last 80 4h bars)

80 × 4h = 13.3 days of price range. Breaking above that = something has changed. Classic Turtle Trader rule. Note: the rolling max excludes the current bar (no peek).

Gate 2 — Slope is genuinely up (the modern addition)
slope of EMA(120) over last 30 bars ≥ +0.03

EMA(120) = ~20 days of average price. Take the LAST 30 bars (~5.0 days) and linear-regress them. The slope (normalized to % of EMA mid-value) must be ≥ +3% over those 30 bars.

This is the slope gate — it kills the classic Turtle failure mode (false breakouts in chop). Donchian on BTC without a slope filter gets killed in 2018, 2022 Q4, etc. With it, the breakouts that fire are nearly all in established trends.

Edge thesis: trend persistence in crypto. When BTC breaks a 13-day high while a 5-day uptrend already exists, the move continues more often than chance. The trade hunts the fat right tail: ~60% of trades hit SL (small loss), but the ~40% that work run 5× ATR ≈ $7,500–$15,000 absolute. At R:R 3.3:1, a 40% hit rate is comfortably profitable. Mirror for shorts.
Failure modes:
  • Whipsaw: price pokes above 80-bar high, slope gate passes, reverses immediately. SL hits within 1-2 bars.
  • Trend exhaustion: breakout was the climax, not the start. SL hits within a few days.
  • Slope misread: bumpy EMA looks trending by linear-regression but isn't really.
  • Time-stop at 8 days: trade exits flat because the trend stalled.

cnh-hybrid-short-v1 Pattern-based short on 4-hour bars

The newest leg, in one sentence

Looks for two specific chart patterns on the 4h timeframe — Distribution Top (DT) and Inverse Cup-and-Handle (ICnH) — and shorts BTC when one fires. Stop-loss is 1.5× ATR(14) above entry; take-profit is the distance down to EMA(100); the trade is skipped if EMA(100) sits above the current close (no valid SHORT TP target). Each pattern is given a single shot per 15-bar (~2.5-day) window — repeats inside that window are ignored, even if they would have fired on their own.

Pattern 1 — Distribution Top (DT)

"Uptrend → chop → breakdown" — classic topping structure on a multi-bar scale.

Rule 1 — Uptrend phase
close[i − 16 − 8] → close[i − 8] rises ≥ 2.5%

Over the 16 bars (= 64 hours ≈ 2.7 days) before the chop window, price rose at least 2.5%. Filters out range-bound noise — DT only makes sense as the exhaustion of a prior up-move.

Rule 2 — Chop phase at the top
range(close, last 8 bars) ≤ 0.55 × range(close, prior uptrend bars)

The last 8 bars (= 32 hours ≈ 1.3 days) drift sideways — narrow range relative to the uptrend that came before. This is the "distribution" — smart money offloading without driving price further up. Optionally requires the chop to actually be at the local peak of the uptrend.

Rule 3 — Breakdown trigger on the firing bar
close[i] < min(chop_lows) OR close[i] < EMA(24)[i]

Current 4h close breaks below either the chop range's low or the 24-bar EMA (= 4 days of price). Either condition counts ("chop_low_or_ema24" mode in the config). The break is the "actually selling now" confirmation — without it, the chop is just chop.

Pattern 2 — Inverse Cup-and-Handle (ICnH)

A bullish-looking cup that fails. The handle's failed retest is the short signal.

Rule 1 — Cup shape (20 bars)
parabolic R² ≥ 0.50 AND depth ≥ 1.0× ATR AND rim heights within ±6 bars

Fit a parabola to the last 20 bars (= 80 hours ≈ 3.3 days). The fit's R² must be ≥ 0.50 (it's actually parabolic, not just noisy), the trough must be at least 1.0 ATR below both rims (real depth), and the two rims must be within ±6 bars of each other (symmetric, not a one-sided slope).

Rule 2 — Handle (4 bars)
handle depth ≤ 70% of cup depth

The 4 bars right after the cup must form a "handle" — a shallower retracement (≤ 70% of the cup's depth). Visually: the cup tries to recover, the handle gives a bit back. Bullish setup — which is exactly why fading it works when the pattern fails.

Rule 3 — EMA-cross trigger (lookback)
close[i] crosses below EMA(24) AND an ICnH pattern was admitted within the last 8 bars

ICnH doesn't fire on the pattern bar itself — it waits up to 8 bars (= 32 hours ≈ 1 days) for a close below EMA(24) to confirm that the cup is failing. If that cross doesn't happen inside the lookback, the setup expires.

Stop, take-profit, and the skip rule

Stop-loss
SL = 1.5 × ATR(14) above entry

Adaptive volatility-scaled stop. At a typical BTC ATR of ~$1,500/4h, that's a ~$2,250 stop — roughly 2-3% of price. Bigger than donchian's stop, smaller than v1's fixed-percent stop.

Take-profit
TP = distance from entry down to EMA(100)

EMA(100) over 4h ≈ 16 days of average price. The trade closes when price retraces back to that mean — variable distance depending on how stretched the breakdown is. Long-tailed trades (where price keeps going) catch the EMA on the way down.

Skip-no-TP rule
if EMA(100) ≥ close: skip the trade entirely

If the take-profit target sits above the entry price, there's no valid downside target — the trade would be opened with TP unreachable. The evaluator returns side=None with reason="dt_admitted_but_no_tp" or "icnh_admitted_but_no_tp". Costs us some signals but the alternative (no take-profit) is unacceptable for a short.

Stateful pattern-level dedup (the load-bearing innovation)

Both DT and ICnH detectors can fire on consecutive bars during a single exhaustion event. Without dedup, the same topping structure produces 4-6 entries back-to-back, all hitting the same drawdown. The Phase 3b validation showed this caused ~40% over-fire versus the backtest's intended behaviour, dragging portfolio Sharpe by -0.36.

The fix: once a pattern is admitted, no further pattern (DT or ICnH) is admitted for 15 bars (= 2.5 days). This is tracked stateful inside the live evaluator by replaying the admission state from the visible bars window each call — matching find_hybrid_patterns's backtest behaviour exactly. Phase 4 portfolio sim confirmed: stateful dedup restores the +0.26 live Sharpe lift that the BTC validation locked in.

Edge thesis: short-only on a long-biased asset is contrarian — most of crypto is in uptrend, so unconditional shorts lose. But topping structures (DT + ICnH) appear only at exhaustion, when the rally is structurally over for a few days. Catching those moments — and only those — gives ~60% win rate at 1.5×ATR vs EMA(100) R:R. Validated in Phase 1 walk-forward with all 3 dedup variants passing the +18.8% / 4-window-positive gate; dedup=15 won on Sharpe and worst-window.
Failure modes:
  • No TP slot: in a downtrend, EMA(100) often sits above current close. Strategy correctly skips, but you lose what would have been valid pattern fires.
  • Strong uptrend resumes: pattern fires, price snaps back, SL hits at 1.5×ATR. Happens ~40% of the time (the lose rate).
  • Pattern-level dedup blocks a real second signal: rare but possible — two genuinely distinct topping structures within 15 bars get reduced to one fire.
  • Cross-coin transfer is uneven: same params on ETH/ADA fail Phase 1; SOL/WLD pass cleanly. The pattern detector is asset-sensitive in a way that v1's RSI/funding gates are not.

Why the trio works

The three strategies are structurally orthogonal on six axes — each occupies a different point in strategy-space. v1 is a short-bar counter-trend trader; donchian-v3 is a long-bar trend-follower; cnh-hybrid-short is a long-bar pattern-fade short.

Axis v1 donchian-v3 cnh-hybrid-short
Timeframe 15m (fast) 4h (slow) 4h (slow)
Signal direction Counter (fade the dip) With (ride the breakout) Counter (fade the top)
Allowed sides Long + short Long + short Short only
Signal logic Indicator confluence Channel breakout + slope Multi-bar pattern shape
R:R 2:1 (symmetric) ~3.3:1 (asymmetric, fat tail) ~2:1 (entry-to-EMA100)
SL method Fixed % of price Adaptive ATR(20) Adaptive ATR(14)
Fire rate ~100/year ~25/year ~9/year

The empirical proof of orthogonality for the v1 ↔ donchian-v3 pair: daily P&L correlation of −0.01 in the realistic 6.7-year simulation (n=2,025 common trading days). The cnh-hybrid-short leg is too new for a live correlation measurement, but its Phase 4 portfolio sim measured a +0.26 Sharpe lift on top of the existing pair — meaning it adds diversification, not redundancy.

Losing strategies that are independent can be combined into a winning portfolio — and winning strategies that are independent compound beyond their individual contributions. A potential 4th leg in this codebase would need correlation < ~0.3 with all three current legs to add real diversification. The Phase-1 cross-coin scan (tools/cross_coin_backtest.py) flagged SOL × cnh-hybrid-short as the strongest candidate (4/4 OOS positive, +42% cum); ETH/ADA on the same strategy didn't transfer cleanly and would need per-coin param tuning.

What runs every 5 seconds (bot loop)

while not halted:
    touch(data/heartbeat)                    # 1. liveness signal
    if exists(data/HALT): flatten(); exit()  # 2. operator kill
    equity = client.fetch_equity_usdt()
    if equity / deploy_start < 0.645: HALT() # 3. -35.5% kill switch

    if NOT in_position:
        bars = client.fetch_ohlcv(timeframe) # 4. recent N bars
        funding = client.fetch_funding()
        decision = evaluate_for_strategy(name, bars, funding, params)
        if decision.side is not None:
            qty = (risk_pct * equity) / decision.sl_distance
            place_bracket_order(side, qty, sl_price, tp_price)

    if in_position and bars_held > time_stop_bars:
        close_position()                     # 5. time stop

    log_to_jsonl()
    push_consolidate_event()                 # 6. dashboard heartbeat
    sleep(poll_interval_s)                   # 5s for v1; 60s for donchian + cnh-short

All three legs run this same loop. The only difference is which evaluate_for_strategy branch executes (selected by strategy_name in the config) and which .env.<instance> file supplies the sub-account API key. The bot is otherwise strategy-agnostic — adding a 4th leg requires only:

  • A new strategy/live_*.py module + dispatch branch in bot_internals.py
  • A new entry in bot.INSTANCE_PROFILES (config / state DB / log / heartbeat paths)
  • A new .env.<instance> with the new sub-account's keys
  • A new entry in tools.watchdog.LEGS
  • A new BotSource union entry in the consolidate web dashboard
